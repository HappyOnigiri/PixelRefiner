Recipes

Each recipe shows a prompt, the image it generated, the Pixel Refiner settings, and the converted result. Each example was generated with the shown prompt and converted with the shown settings.

Recipe 1: Game character sprite

GoalThe basic form of a transparent asset. The themes are how to choose the background color (a warm-colored character against a magenta background) and leaving margin so the subject is never cropped. 

Pixel art sprite of a knight character, full body, front view, standing pose,
16-bit retro game style, chunky pixels, limited color palette, flat shading,
centered with margin around the character,
full frame edge-to-edge solid magenta background (#FF00FF), no drop shadow, no outer glow, no gradient

Pixel Refiner settingsLeave Preset on “Best Match” (the background is made transparent automatically).

Recipe 2: Item icon

GoalA single object for UI use. Pairing a red subject with a green background makes the two easy to separate for clean background removal. 

Pixel art of a red potion bottle, single centered object, low resolution,
flat colors, limited palette, full frame edge-to-edge solid bright green background (#00FF00),
chroma key background fills entire canvas, completely green from edge to edge,
no borders, no margins, no frame, no white background, no shadow, no reflection

Pixel Refiner settingsChoose the “Transparent Icon” preset. It removes the background and reduces the image to 32 colors.

Recipe 3: Retro handheld look (Game Boy palette)

GoalAsk for few tones at the generation stage, then let palette conversion map them exactly onto the four tones of the console you choose. The generated colors themselves do not survive, so the background is specified for easy removal — red against a green subject — rather than for the finished look. 

Pixel art sprite of a small dragon, Game Boy style, 4-color monochrome green palette,
low resolution, chunky pixels, full frame edge-to-edge solid red background (#FF0000)

Pixel Refiner settingsChoose the “Retro Game Style” preset. The background is made transparent automatically.

Recipe 4: Scenery for a background

GoalA case without transparency. A landscape with no characters or props is turned into pixel art you can use as a background as it is. The key is asking for few elements and large pixels. 

Pixel art background illustration for an 8-bit retro JRPG, NES style, wide grassland with distant mountains under a clear morning sky, scenery only, no characters, no creatures, no objects, no buildings, no items, ultra low-res 64x64 pixel art scaled up, extremely chunky visible square pixels, simple composition, strict limited 8-color palette, strict retro pixel grid, sharp square edges, hard dithering, absolutely no anti-aliasing, no smooth gradients, no blur, no text, no UI, no frame

Pixel Refiner settingsChoose the “Background Artwork” preset.

Recipe 5: Turn a normal illustration into pixel art

GoalEven with models or styles that cannot generate pixel art directly, an ordinary illustration can be converted into pixel art. Flat coloring and thick outlines survive the conversion well, so ask for them instead of asking for pixel art. 

Cute chibi character illustration, cel shading, flat colors, thick outlines,
full frame edge-to-edge solid blue background, no gradients, no drop shadow

Pixel Refiner settingsChoose the “Convert Illustration to Pixel Art” preset. To make the pixels coarser or finer, adjust Pixel Detail in Quick Settings afterward.
